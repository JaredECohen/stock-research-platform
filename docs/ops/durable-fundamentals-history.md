# Durable fundamentals history

`fundamental_history_service.backfill_fundamentals(ticker, start_date, force_refresh=False)` fills the existing `financial_periods` table without fetching filings/transcripts, creating companies, or invoking LLMs. It requests both annual and quarterly FMP statements, with a request size derived from the requested date rather than the normal eight-annual-period limit. Alpha Vantage fallback reads its explicit `annualReports` and `quarterlyReports` collections. Normal statement APIs retain their cadence and payload shape. Providers without a dedicated, cadence-aware history adapter are reported as unavailable rather than sending an ambiguous generic payload into history. A provider that returns partial required coverage does not suppress later providers; all attempts and remaining gaps are reported. Share-class aliases use the existing symbol-variant policy. Fundamentals request canonical `BK`; the BNY rename applies to the separately verified price route, not historical financial statements.

Rows retain the actual provider, reported currency, fiscal period/end, fetch timestamp and availability rule. The natural key remains ticker/period/statement/line item. Missing, boolean and nonfinite numeric values cannot replace usable values. Known-provider currency/value conflicts retain the stored value and identify the statement, period and line; equal-value corroboration preserves the original provider. A same-provider restatement may update a value, keeping its existing availability date; this table is **latest-restated data, not an as-originally-reported revision ledger**. A validated named provider may replace legacy `live`/unknown/demo currency metadata and fill NULL values at a matching period end, with old/new source, value and currency identities in `source_upgrades`; since the FMP-primary change (below) a legacy *value* that FMP contradicts is quarantined instead of overwritten in place. Older ingestion omitted currency and therefore stored the model's USD default even for foreign statements; replacing that unprovenanced default with reported currency is a metadata correction, not FX conversion. Missing period ends and availability can be filled; two different known period ends are a conflict. Legacy ingests without a real provider identity cannot later replace a known provider's value while falsely retaining its provenance.

FMP debt fields preserve reported zero separately from missing data. Reported `totalDebt` is preferred; a total is derived only when both short- and long-term components are known. A missing component is never assumed to be zero. Zero common dividends also remain zero instead of falling through to a different dividends field. Legitimate stored NULL optional lines remain in the database, are excluded from numerical reads, and retain full identities as `missing_stored_optional_value` warnings. The shared blocker rule treats those warnings consistently in public coverage, database-only skip eligibility and backfill success, preventing endless refreshes. Missing primary values, nonfinite values, malformed periods and missing currency on actual numeric facts remain blockers.

Currency validation applies to incoming statements, stored numerical reads, coverage and authoritative replacement candidates. Codes must contain three ASCII letters after normalization; placeholder values including `None`, `NULL`, `N/A`, `NAN`, `UNKNOWN`, `XXX` and `XTS` are rejected. This is a format/sentinel check, not an FX conversion or a claim that every possible three-letter code has been verified against an ISO registry. Invalid incoming currencies retain provider, symbol, statement, period, date and currency identities in the report; stored invalid facts remain excluded and identified.

The public `fundamental_coverage` helper reads the database only. It reports annual and quarterly coverage for income, balance and cash, keyed to revenue, total assets and operating cash flow respectively. Complete coverage requires a dated observation at/before the requested start within the freshness allowance, no missing fiscal periods through the newest observation, and a newest period no older than 460 days (annual), 140 days (latest Q1/Q2/Q4), or 185 days (latest Q3, allowing for the annual report that supplies Q4). `stale_threshold_days` exposes the applied allowance. These are operational freshness guards, not an exchange or regulatory filing calendar, and do not guarantee every optional line item exists; rejected/missing primary values are named in the backfill report. Short histories, new listings and provider plan limits remain gaps rather than silently satisfying a two-year requirement. Demo and unprovenanced rows cannot establish coverage. An explicit refresh also requires complete coverage from newly fetched payloads: usable stored history cannot turn failed or partial fresh calls into a successful refresh. `refresh_complete` distinguishes the two.

Freshness means holding the latest period the issuer has **reported**, not a recent fetch (FIX-005, owner decision 2026-09-24: "fundamental data should load as often as it changes"; the earlier seven-day `refresh_ttl_days` / `stored_fetch_stale` contract is gone). Each bucket exposes `expected_latest_period_end` and `expected_basis` (`edgar_filing`: the newest 10-Q/10-K, or 20-F/40-F for the annual cadence, that EDGAR lists, from `fundamental_refresh_state`), `expected_evidence` (form, accession, filing date, when it was observed, and `missing_after`), `period_current` (the newest stored period is within the 7-day 52/53-week tolerance of it), `awaiting_provider` (not current but still inside `PUBLICATION_GRACE = 72h` from observation) and `fresh` (= not overdue). A complete bucket that is overdue is the blocking issue `expected_period_missing` with the filed period's identity. `latest_primary_fetched_at` remains and means "last provider confirmation"; fetch age alone never blocks and never forces a provider call. A same-provider verification of identical facts still refreshes its fetch timestamp (counted in `rows_written` and separately in `rows_refreshed`); a different provider's equal-value corroboration does not. Failed refreshes preserve usable stored values, fetch times and availability.

An issuer that stopped reporting (acquisition/delisting) is listed in `KNOWN_REPORTING_ENDED` with a primary source (ANSS, AVB, EA). Its coverage is judged over the same span ending on that date (`coverage_end`; `coverage_start` moves back by the same span, so it does not turn blocking once the requested start passes its last period) and no newer period is expected. The response carries `reporting_ended_on`, `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`. An EDGAR 25/25-NSE/15-* notice never ends an expectation: it can delist one class of notes of an issuer that keeps filing, so it is only recorded (`last_deregistration_form`) as evidence.

`read_stored_financials` reconstructs statement rows with source, per-line source and currency metadata, newest first. Fiscal labels must have a four-digit year and optional Q1–Q4; ISO dates are not fiscal labels. Before enumerating gaps, both incoming and stored years must agree with the actual period-end calendar year within one year, and future dates are rejected. Gap enumeration uses only the requested date window and a fresh preceding anchor. Invalid stored identities appear in `_history_issues`; conflicting dates, currencies or values exclude the whole statement period. Equivalent labels such as `2025` and `FY2025` merge only when facts agree. A single legacy alias can be updated without inserting a duplicate; multiple stored aliases are identified and blocked from arbitrary restatement. Conflicting provider duplicates are also rejected, with every occurrence identified. Use `cadence="annual"` when falling back for existing annual-only valuation/ratio consumers; the default returns both cadences for history inspection. It never seeds a table or reaches a provider. Backfill owns its transaction by default; when supplied a session, it only flushes and reports `committed=false`, leaving commit/rollback to the caller.

Alpha Vantage does not supply the same fiscal labels as FMP. Its adapter derives quarterly labels from annual fiscal-end anchors, tolerating 52/53-week date shifts; only the next fiscal year may use an extrapolated annual end. Missing, future or ambiguous anchors cannot establish a quarter. Each accepted derived period has a `fiscal_period_derivation` report entry with provider, symbol, statement, period, period end, basis and anchor date. This audit is in the backfill report, not a new database column or an original fiscal-calendar ledger; retain the report with the backfill evidence.

Legacy fiscal labels can be corrected only when a dedicated provider history response supplies an explicit fiscal label for the exact stored period end, statement, line item and cadence. The request extends to the oldest stored legacy period end, reported as `provider_requested_start`, so an entire shifted chain can be checked against provider evidence. Inferred labels cannot authorize a move. A complete destination preflight protects known-provider facts and ambiguous destinations; approved chains or cycles use temporary unique keys inside a transaction. Row IDs and availability dates are preserved. `period_relabels` records the old/new labels, fiscal metadata, values, source and dates; the subsequent named-provider refresh is separately recorded in `source_upgrades` for the same row ID. `rows_relabelled` overlaps ordinary writes and must not be added to them as distinct rows. A caller-owned session can still roll back every relabel and refresh.

The provider request lookback and required coverage boundary are separate. `_fetch_financial_history(..., required_start=start_date)` may fetch earlier legacy-label evidence while deciding whether another provider is needed against the user's actual `start_date`. Missing prelisting or older optional history does not trigger fallback when the first provider already covers all six required buckets. This prevents an expanded legacy lookback from producing unnecessary cross-provider conflicts for ARM, PLTR or SNOW. Older discrepancies remain reported and preserved; the requested coverage floor is not silently widened or shortened.

Blocked optional NULL collisions remain stored with a named warning. A populated legacy duplicate can also remain unchanged while a confirmed replacement supplies usable history: exactly one valid numeric named-provider fact must match its period end, statement, line and cadence under a different valid fiscal label. `legacy_duplicate_observation_excluded` retains both complete identities, values, currencies, sources and date metadata. Only a destination-collision relabel issue for that same independently excluded row becomes `legacy_period_relabel_preserved_duplicate`; ambiguous labels, known-provider contradictions and unsupported collisions remain blocking. This handles LULU's populated legacy FY2026 debt rows beside confirmed FY2025 facts at the same observed year-end without deleting, relabeling or overwriting the preserved duplicates.

Other conflicting duplicate period-end groups remain excluded from numerical reads. Normal FMP income, balance and cash mappers also honor a valid explicit `fiscalYear`, preventing later routine ingestion from recreating calendar-year labels for noncalendar fiscal years. If the provider omits a declared fiscal quarter, the prior calendar-quarter fallback remains unchanged rather than inventing a fiscal quarter.

Invalid or undated legacy aliases such as raw `2023` can coexist with a provider-confirmed `FY2023` fact. The alias row, ID, value, date fields and provenance remain unchanged; no missing date is inferred. A separately keyed canonical fact may be inserted or refreshed without choosing the unusable alias as the update target. Public reads retain the excluded alias's full identity as `unusable_legacy_observation`, with the usable canonical row ID/source/date. This warning becomes nonblocking only after a valid numeric named-provider canonical fact exists for the same statement and line. Missing canonical facts, known-provider contradictions and invalid exact-key occupants within an alias collision group remain blocking. The prior standalone legacy canonical missing-end upgrade remains supported.

`FinancialPeriod` keeps its existing columns and unique index. The separate BK reconciliation workflow adds the `financial_data_repairs` audit table; it saves a provider-derived plan before any financial mutation and applies only its reviewed digest against exact before-image and destination fences. See [the admin route contract and quarantine-count limitation](durable-market-data.md#bk-reviewed-plan-reconciliation) and [the full reconciliation rules](bk-fundamental-reconciliation.md). Preparation uses one canonical BK provider pass; GET and apply use the saved plan, with no new provider requests. Unmatched rows retain their IDs and all data under a reserved noncompany `~Q` namespace, with full original identities in the audit. Normal target coverage counts exclude that namespace and must be supplemented with quarantine counts when reporting a physical database total.

These notes describe code `881049b`; they do not claim production repair application without its saved receipt. No Company, research prompt, DCF assumption, historical memo, snapshot or outcome row is changed by the BK repair. The ordinary fundamental service does not generate memos or evaluate outcomes.

## FMP primary (owner decision 2026-09-24, FIX-006)

FMP is the primary fundamentals provider everywhere; other providers only fill periods FMP does not report. Conflicting rows are quarantined, never deleted. The paragraphs above about preserved legacy duplicates and undated aliases still describe **reads**; for any period FMP reports, a backfill now moves those rows aside as described here.

**Ownership rule.** FMP owns every observation it reports. An equal FMP verification *adopts* a secondary or legacy observation in place (source becomes `fmp`, fetch time now, id and availability kept). An FMP disagreement *quarantines* the other row and stores FMP's observation. Secondary providers still cannot date-stamp or adopt an FMP observation and never fill a line inside a period FMP reports (the whole row is skipped as `secondary_rows_skipped`, each differing line named as nonblocking `secondary_disagrees_with_primary`). Between two non-primary providers the earlier rules are unchanged (`stored_value_conflict` stays blocking).

**Quarantine reasons** (first match wins, evaluated after the legacy relabel):

| Reason | Stored row (never FMP's own) | Known case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `alias_superseded_by_primary` | a different raw label of a period FMP reports (`2023` beside `FY2023`) | MSFT, NVDA bare-year aliases |
| `period_end_conflicts_with_primary` | exact key, different period end | MDT FY2018 (2018-04-30 vs 2018-04-27) |
| `value_conflicts_with_primary` | exact key, different finite value (legacy drift included) | ANSS AV rows |
| `currency_conflicts_with_primary` | named secondary, exact key, different currency | review list |
| `invalid_availability_precedes_period_end` | exact key, availability before FMP's period end | |
| `label_conflicts_with_primary_observation` | own label unknown to FMP, but FMP observes the same line within 7 days of its period end | LULU legacy FY2026 debt |
| `secondary_row_in_primary_period` | named secondary line FMP does not report, inside a period FMP reports | ARM/PLTR/SNOW in-period rows |

Not quarantined: equal legacy rows (upgraded in place), legacy NULL values (filled), legacy currency-only differences (the documented metadata correction), and legacy lines FMP does not report (they came from the same FMP-first chain; missing never erases). Pre-listing periods FMP lacks keep their secondary rows.

**Legacy drift policy.** A legacy row whose value differs from FMP is quarantined and FMP's row inserted carrying the legacy row's availability when that date is valid (not before the period end). This supersedes the 2026-09-13 contract that a verified refresh upgrades legacy values in place, and the relabel id-preservation claim for drifted rows: a relabelled row whose value FMP confirms keeps its id; a drifted one is quarantined at its provider-confirmed label (the audit keeps the pre-run label as `pre_run`).

**LULU 35607/35609.** FMP's own rows whose stored availability precedes their period end are *flagged* (nonblocking `primary_availability_precedes_period_end`, `action: flagged_not_moved`), not moved and not re-inserted, so the PIT exclusion in [scorecard PIT eligibility](scorecard-pit-eligibility.md) keeps holding. `history_service._upsert_financial_period` now refuses (and reports `period_end_change_refused`) any `period_end` change on a row that already carries `available_at`; that is how an INSERT-only availability ended up before its period end.

**Mechanics.** `fundamental_quarantine.quarantine_rows` moves rows with a fenced Core `UPDATE ... WHERE id AND ticker AND <every judged field>`; a row changed after it was read aborts and rolls back the whole ticker (`QuarantineFenceError`). One reserved 16-character `~Q` namespace per ticker-run; one `financial_data_repairs` audit (`kind: fmp_primary_takeover`) with complete before-images, reasons, FMP's fact and, after the write, `replacement_ids`. Adoptions and same-provider value restatements are audited the same way (`primary_adoption`, `restatement`, before- and after-images). `fundamental_quarantine.restore_repair(repair_id)` (script: `python -m app.scripts.fundamentals_quarantine_restore <repair_id>`) reverses any of the three: a quarantine restore first moves the current occupants (FMP's replacements) into a `restore_displaced` quarantine with its own audit; an in-place restore requires each row to still match its after-image (a later FMP restatement makes it refuse rather than overwrite the newer value). An adoption's before-image is the row as it was before the run, so restoring an adoption also reverses a relabel the same run made; whatever now occupies the old key (FMP's own row for that label) is moved into a `restore_displaced` quarantine first. A run that fails and rolls back reports empty `quarantined`/`adoptions`/`restatements`/`source_upgrades`/`period_relabels` lists, with what it had planned under `rolled_back_plan`, so no consumer counts changes that never persisted. Report fields: `rows_quarantined`, `quarantine_repair_id`, `quarantined[]`, `adoptions[]`, `restatements[]`, `adoption_repair_id`, `restatement_repair_id`, `secondary_rows_skipped`, `rows_inserted`, `new_period_ends`.

**Modes.** `backfill_fundamentals(..., dry_run=True)` runs everything inside a transaction and rolls it back: the report is the exact plan and nothing persists (owned session required). `mode="supervised"` (admin re-pull) applies at once. `mode="unattended"` (for scheduled refreshes) applies only when the set is at most `QUARANTINE_AUTO_MAX_ROWS = 20` rows and 5% of the ticker's rows **and** every reason is value, currency or availability; otherwise it writes a `FinancialDataRepair(status="planned")`, moves nothing, blocks FMP's writes to those keys and reports blocking `primary_takeover_planned`. Review it with `GET /api/admin/market-data/bk-repair/{id}` and apply with `POST .../bk-repair/{id}/apply` (digest- and before-image-fenced); the next refresh inserts FMP's rows. `expected_plan` (a reviewed dry run's `plan_identity`: quarantine ids and reasons, adoption ids, restatement values and relabels by row id, and whether FMP answered) makes an executed re-pull abort the whole ticker (`repull_plan_mismatch`, `differs` naming the parts) when any part differs. Inserts of periods nothing stores are not compared: they overwrite nothing.

**Reads (R1-R3).** `read_stored_financials` prefers FMP: a legacy row with several named replacements uses the single FMP one (R3); among several labels at one period end, the only label group holding FMP rows wins and the others are named as nonblocking `label_superseded_by_primary` with every identity and value (R2); inside a group with FMP rows, named-secondary rows and legacy rows that FMP covers or that disagree on period end or currency are dropped as nonblocking `superseded_by_primary` (R1). Conflicts among FMP rows stay blocking.

**Definition breaks.** BK is stored exactly as FMP reports it. `KNOWN_DEFINITION_BREAKS` flags 2026Q1 revenue (gross of interest expense) and 2026Q2 diluted EPS and share count as nonblocking `provider_definition_break` with the evidence path, only while the stored value is within 0.5% of the evidenced provider value, so an FMP correction retires the flag.

**Provider order and entitlement.** `data_service._try_chain_symbol` is provider-major: FMP is asked under every spelling before a fallback is asked. `FMPProvider._get_status(path, **params) -> (status, body)` logs `FMP <path> -> <status> symbol=<symbol>` (never the key); history calls record 401/402/403 as `provider_entitlement_denied {status, endpoint, statement, cadence}` instead of `provider_no_data`, and a denial under one spelling that FMP answers under another (BRK.B -> BRK-B) is marked `resolved_by_symbol`. Only unresolved denials indicate a plan gap. The provider request window reaches back to the oldest stored non-FMP row, named secondaries included.

**Legacy daily path retired for provider-owned tickers.** For a ticker that already has named-provider rows (`"fundamentals": "durable"` in its result), `history_service.backfill_ticker` makes no statements read and writes no `financial_periods` (`financial_periods: 0`). That anonymous, cache-backed annual read cannot say which provider answered or whether a cache did. New periods arrive through the filing-driven refresh below. First-contact and demo tickers keep the legacy ingest, and both introduction paths (`routes_stocks._ensure_lazy_universe`, `regen_worker._introduce_ticker`) also request a durable import (`first_contact`). Decision 7(d): nothing here adds `revenue_growth` to `ratios`.

## Refresh on filing (FIX-005, W5a S6)

`services/fundamental_refresh.py`, state in `fundamental_refresh_state` (one row per ticker; Postgres, because the poller, the nightly drain, the regen pull-through and admin syncs run in different threads and processes).

- **Detection.** `edgar_poller` (every 30 min, in-tier issuers) hands every index it read to `observe_many` once per pass. The SEC metadata index now also lists up to 10 amendments / 20-F / 40-F rows and, under their own cap of 5, deregistration notices, beside an unchanged first-ten 10-K/10-Q/8-K body window (so the poller's seen-set cannot re-fire on deploy; extras never produce filing events). `observe_many` records the newest filed quarterly and annual period ends and schedules a `filing` refresh only when the filed period is not stored (by more than the 7-day tolerance), or `first_import` when nothing named is stored. "Stored" is judged the way coverage judges it: the filed period must be in all three statements' primary lines (revenue, total assets, operating cash flow) with a valid currency and a finite value, so an FMP publication that has the income statement but not yet the balance sheet or cash flow stays a pending filing retry. A deploy against a fully imported database schedules nothing. A later amendment schedules one re-verification (`amendment`; the first observation only records it). A new filed period upgrades a pending calendar/sweep/amendment/admin request to `filing`.
- **Nightly drain** (inside `history_backfill`, 03:15 UTC; no new loop, `KNOWN_LOOPS` unchanged): `schedule_calendar_and_sweep` over **every** `companies` row except ETFs (every `fundamentals_required` target, not only the polled tier; an ETF files no statements, and `request` refuses one): a company with no named rows gets one `first_import` (also when an admin sync left a bare idle state row; not again once one was abandoned), an overdue period (anchored on the statement that lags) (last quarter + 91 days + 45/90 days, 60/120 for 20-F/40-F filers, + 7) is re-checked at most weekly (`calendar`), and a ticker whose newest provider confirmation is 120 days old is re-confirmed (`sweep`). Then `drain` runs up to 30 due refreshes (15-minute budget, 1 s pacing) in `(filing, first_import/first_contact, amendment/admin, calendar, sweep)` then due-time order; everything over either bound is named. Each refresh is `backfill_fundamentals(force_refresh=True, mode="unattended")` under a 15-minute per-ticker lease that the admin sync and the pull-through share.
- **Schedule, in nights.** First check at least 6 h after the filing is seen (night 1), retries on nights 2, 3, 5 and 9 (+12 h, +24 h, +48 h, +96 h; a due time within an hour after 03:00 UTC is rounded down to that drain slot), then weekly until 45 days after the first check. An abandoned row keeps its `first_due_at` and attempt count, so a weekly `calendar` check (one FMP call a week, once the stored period is overdue by the calendar) goes straight back to idle instead of restarting the retries; only a newer filed period (observed by the poller) starts a new cycle. The night-9 attempt may let Alpha Vantage fill exactly the filed period FMP still lacks. The FMP publication lag is unmeasured; `last_result` records every attempt so it can be tuned after one earnings season.
- **Outcome is derived from stored state.** After every run (drain, pull-through or admin), `record_result` re-reads the newest stored periods and the filed ones: if a filed period is still missing the row stays (or goes back to) pending as a filing retry, keeping its first due time and attempt count (only drain/pull-through retries count as attempts; an admin run does not clear or advance it). A request that arrived during the run is kept. The write is a compare-and-set on `row_version` that re-reads and recomputes on conflict, so a filing observed mid-run is never lost.
- **Cache invalidation.** When a committed refresh wrote new values (not only timestamps) or quarantined rows, the `financials` provider-cache row and the ticker's `company_cold` snapshot are invalidated: that is the moment FMP actually published. Outcomes are in `last_result.cache_invalidation`.
- **Stuck policy.** A ticker still missing its filed period after the night-9 attempt (or abandoned at 45 days) fails the `history_backfill` run on the night it becomes stuck (`fundamentals stuck ...`), and afterwards is named under `fundamentals still missing` without failing it. A lagging period inside its retry window is named under `fundamentals missing filed periods` and is not a failure. Other note segments: `fund_refreshed=N fund_pending=P`, `fundamentals over the 30-refresh cap`, `over the 900s drain budget`, `leased elsewhere`, `quarantined=N: T:<repair_id>`, `fmp entitlement denied` (unresolved 401/402/403 only), `fundamentals failed`, and in CI/demo `fundamentals refresh skipped: no named history provider in the financials chain`.
- **Pull-through.** A memo regen job runs `refresh_if_due` for its ticker right before the memo (only when already pending and due), so a memo started after FMP published but before 03:15 gets the new period. It never requests a memo.
- **Visibility.** The admin coverage report attaches `fundamentals_refresh` (state summary) per company; the Explorer's provenance says "FY ending <end> was filed (<form>, <date>) and is not stored yet" once a filed fiscal year is past its publication grace.
- **Production data.** Nothing here needs an operator: the table is created by `create_all`, the poller fills it, and the worker drains it. Unattended quarantines stay within S5's limits (`SAFE_AUTO_REASONS`, 20 rows / 5%); anything else becomes a planned repair for review.
