## High-Quality Compounders

Objective: preserve and compound capital through durable, high-return businesses that steadily compound earnings and cash flow over long periods.

- Sustainably high profitability and returns on investment: demonstrated strong returns on capital across cycles; ROIC typically above 15–20%, stable or improving.
- Durable brand and competitive moat: pricing power, customer loyalty, and barriers to entry that strengthen over time; examples include brand, switching costs, scale advantages, and regulatory moats.
- Consistent above-market growth: steady revenue and earnings growth driven by productive reinvestment rather than leverage.
- Strong balance sheet and disciplined leverage: conservative capital structure and flexibility to invest through downturns.
- Reasonable valuation relative to durability: willing to pay for quality only when long-term prospective returns remain attractive. Seek entry prices supporting mid-teens or higher IRRs without heroic assumptions.

## Potential 100-Baggers

- Small starting size: small- or micro-cap, ideally below $5 billion market capitalization and often below $1–2 billion, with substantial asymmetry still available.
- A 10–20-year reinvestment runway: obvious, repeatable opportunities to deploy capital at high returns.
- High incremental ROIC: each new dollar invested improves the business, rather than merely enlarging it.
- Asset-light, scalable economics: growth need not require proportionately more people, factories, or capital.
- Competitive strength increases with scale: switching costs, network effects, data, or brand deepen over time.
- Low dilution and low leverage: growth funded internally and an uncomplicated, resilient balance sheet.
- Owner-operator leadership: founder or CEO behaves like a long-term owner rather than a hired manager.
- “Boring early, obvious late”: ignored, niche, or misunderstood businesses at small scale.
- Emerging pricing power: customers accept higher prices without leaving.
- Ability to survive mistakes: one bad quarter or product should not threaten the entire business.

## High-Growth Bets

Objective: capture asymmetric upside from emerging platform leaders with the potential to become dominant businesses over the long term, even before they are current market leaders.

- Prioritize rapid revenue growth, user adoption, and ecosystem expansion over near-term profitability; revenue growth typically above 25–30%, driven by adoption and scale.
- Seek asymmetric opportunities where long-term upside materially outweighs downside risk.
- Look for category leadership in formation, even while economics are maturing.
- Require a clear path to monetization and operating leverage: improving unit economics and identifiable monetization optionality.
- Seek durable structural advantages: network effects, accumulated data, ecosystem lock-in, switching costs, or brand-driven habit formation.
- Favor founder-led or visionary, long-term-oriented management willing to prioritize platform value over near-term earnings.
- Accept short-term volatility for long-term optionality and platform scale, including holding through business-model and monetization inflection points.

## Investment process

1. Screen financials to generate initial ideas.
2. Conduct qualitative analysis: describe the product and business, including revenue breakdown; assess the competitive landscape and the company's position; evaluate business strategy and growth outlook.
3. Conduct quantitative analysis: examine growth history and forecasts, profitability trends, and financial health; value the business using multiples compared with both its own history and peers, alongside a DCF model.
