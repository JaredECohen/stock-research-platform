## Governing research hierarchy — causal long-term compounding

The primary long-term objective is durable growth in intrinsic value **per diluted share**, not growth in company size, reported revenue or a favored KPI by itself. Build a long-term compounder thesis from the outside in, in this order:

**World change → industry consequences → durable company advantage → value capture → reinvestment runway → long-term per-share compounding → financial evidence/falsification → valuation.**

This is the required thesis-construction order. Evidence should test every link as the work proceeds; the seventh stage is the formal financial audit, not permission to postpone verification. For each arrow, state the causal mechanism, expected time horizon, necessary conditions, supporting evidence, plausible competing explanation and falsifier. Reason from external structural changes—such as technology and cost curves, regulation, demographics, geopolitics, customer behavior or industry capacity—into future industry structure and company economics. A broad theme is not yet a company thesis.

| Stage | Required question |
|---:|---|
| 1. World change | What durable external change is occurring, why is it happening and over what horizon? |
| 2. Industry consequences | How does it alter demand, supply, cost curves, bargaining power, regulation, capital intensity, competitive structure or the location of the profit pool? |
| 3. Durable company advantage | Why is this company positioned to benefit, and why should that advantage survive competition, substitution and supply response? |
| 4. Value capture | Through what mechanism does the advantage become retained revenue, margins, cash flow or lower risk—and how much value is kept after customers, suppliers, employees, regulators and other stakeholders respond? |
| 5. Reinvestment runway | Where can the company redeploy capital, at what incremental return, with what funding requirement and for how long before returns decay? |
| 6. Long-term per-share compounding | What can owner earnings, free cash flow and intrinsic value per diluted share compound to after necessary reinvestment, dilution, debt, acquisitions, dividends and repurchases? |
| 7. Financial evidence/falsification | Which dated filings, operating data, cash reconciliations and counter-evidence support or break each prior link? What observation would force the thesis to change? |
| 8. Valuation | What expectations are embedded in price, what return and downside follow from the causal scenarios, and what margin of safety remains? |

### Numbers are evidence and checkpoints, not the thesis

Use numbers to establish base rates, test each causal link, bound magnitude and timing, expose contradictions, distinguish an attractive business from an attractive security and define falsifiers. Keep **observed facts**, **causal inferences** and **forecast assumptions** visibly separate. Forecasts must be outputs of the causal reasoning above. If financial or operating evidence contradicts a necessary link, revise or reject the thesis rather than forcing the model to preserve it.

### Anti-pattern: KPI-first research

Do **not** use **current KPI → forecast KPI → EPS → target price** as the primary idea-generation or underwriting process. That sequence can create false precision while leaving unanswered why the KPI should grow, who captures the value, why the economics persist, what capital is required and whether value compounds per share. KPIs, EPS forecasts, scenarios and target prices remain useful downstream—as tests, measurement, falsification, expectations analysis and valuation—but they must not substitute for the causal thesis.

The separate shorter-term inflection mandate remains valid. Apply the same causal discipline on its shorter horizon: identify the external or company-specific change, the transmission into economics, the evidence that the market is mispricing it and the event or observation that would falsify it.

## Required company evidence standard — clarified September 10, 2026

The user explicitly emphasizes 10-Ks, quarterly earnings reports and management discussion as the foundation for understanding updated performance, upcoming risks and current conditions. Apply this requirement to subsequent company investigations.

- **Latest annual filing:** read the business and segment discussion, MD&A, material risk factors, audited statements and relevant notes on revenue, working capital, debt, leases, acquisitions, equity compensation and other material claims. Compare prior-year disclosures where needed to identify what changed. An annual earnings release does not replace the annual filing.
- **Latest quarterly evidence:** pair the actual 10-Q with the quarterly earnings release, non-GAAP reconciliation and management discussion/call. For foreign issuers use the applicable annual and interim equivalents. A release, transcript, filing and presentation are different documents and must be recorded separately.
- **Performance explanation:** bridge revenue through volume, realized price, mix, currency and transactions; bridge margins through operating changes, temporary items and accounting; bridge earnings into cash after investment and financing. Record management's explanation as a claim and check it against statements, notes and industry evidence.
- **Forward risks and state:** identify changed risk language, demand/customer behavior, backlog quality, inventory, cost pressure, capital plans, debt maturities, covenants, dilution, regulatory milestones and guidance assumptions. Record the next observable test and plausible competing explanations.
- **Traceability:** save issuer/ticker, filing type, fiscal period, publication/access date, source URL, sections actually reviewed, findings and open questions. Label partial reading explicitly; only mark a full-document read when the whole document has actually been examined.
- **Coverage claims:** a company name or listing match is a research lead. Industry-framework coverage is separate from filing-backed company coverage. A selected-section review is separate from full reading and from a valuation-ready thesis. Do not treat links located but unread, failed retrievals or duplicated filings as additional company reviews.

Use this standard during the next depth investigations. The historical count below does not certify that all 16 companies already meet the expanded evidence standard.

## How to move from a mapped industry to an investment decision

1. **Establish the actual business and security.** Verify issuer name, current listing, share class, domicile, segment mix, transaction perimeter and fiscal period. The example companies here are economic comparisons, not licensed issuer GICS assignments. A foreign or unresolved security is a research lead until eligibility is checked.
2. **Draw the cash mechanism.** Identify customer, payer, unit, price, variable cost, fixed cost, invested capital and collection lag. Separate principal-versus-agent revenue, owned assets versus royalties, and regulated versus merchant risk.
3. **Build a primary evidence packet.** Read the latest annual filing's business, segments, risks and notes; the latest interim; material event filings; earnings reconciliation; and management discussion. Add the relevant industry data and one genuinely comparable peer. Examine a downturn or prior cycle where available.
4. **Reconcile earnings and funding.** Match business perimeter and dates across income, cash, debt, leases, preferreds, minorities, acquisitions and diluted shares. A financing benefit can be real without being operating improvement. Never count a credit facility and its backstop twice.
5. **Compare scenarios with expectations.** Model the economic driver that could differ from the market, a plausible downside, and what is already in price. A management forecast reproduced exactly is not an independent edge. Use a matched current price only after the cash and share bridge closes.
6. **Write the next observable test.** Specify the metric, source, period, expected direction, alternate explanation and falsifier. Save the observation when it arrives and explain whether it changes valuation or confidence.
7. **Choose a disposition.** Deepen, conditional price watch, event watch, pass, or evidence unavailable. Move the breadth lane to a new model when the decisive evidence is not yet available; do not repeat a familiar thesis as if it were fresh coverage.

### Valuation discipline across different models

For an operating business, begin with normalized cash after necessary reinvestment and all relevant claims. Revenue growth creates value only when incremental returns justify the capital and risk. Growth, reinvestment rate and returns on incremental capital must be mutually consistent.

For a lender or insurer, cash-flow-statement “free cash flow” is usually the wrong starting point. Estimate sustainable earnings after credit/claim costs and the capital required to support risk, then determine what can actually reach common shareholders. For a REIT, reconcile FFO to cash after property upkeep and leasing. For a utility, rate-base growth must be paired with funding and diluted shares. For a drug developer, clinical probabilities and financing runway precede any mature earnings multiple.

Avoid universal valuation cutoffs. Compare a conservative range of values with price and the probability of financing failure or dilution. Sensitivity to a cap rate, credit loss, commodity deck or terminal margin should be visible. A low multiple of peak profit or a high yield funded by asset erosion is not automatically an opportunity.

### 10101010 — Oil & Gas Drilling

**Hierarchy:** Energy → Energy → Energy Equipment & Services.

**Company research leads:** HP; RIG; PTEN.

**Security check:** Provider-observed US symbols: HP, RIG, PTEN. These are economic comparisons; confirm current issuer, listing and segment perimeter before use.

**Economics and submodels:** Land rigs earn day rates on contracted working days; offshore rigs have longer mobilization and contracting cycles. Separate owned rigs, labor, reimbursables, idle assets and performance bonuses.

**Advantage and supply response:** Efficient crews and scarce high-specification rigs can protect returns; stacked rigs are latent competing supply. A long backlog is useful only after termination clauses, customer quality and reactivation cost.

**Monitor and cadence:** Weekly rig counts and tenders; quarterly utilization, day rates, backlog conversion and idle cash burn. Watch renewal pricing rather than average fleet rates alone.

**Cash, accounting and valuation traps:** Deduct maintenance, surveys and reactivation investment. EBITDA can rise while debt service and recommissioning consume cash; depreciated book value is not replacement economics.

**Opportunity and falsifier:** Inflection: better-priced renewals reach cash before meaningful new capacity returns. Falsifier: customers defer wells or reactivated supply destroys pricing.

**Next decisive investigation:** Compare HP land-fleet renewal economics with RIG offshore backlog, calculating contract-level cash after activation and the liquidity required before first revenue.

**Related mechanisms:** Oil and gas value chain.

### 10102030 — Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing

**Hierarchy:** Energy → Energy → Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels.

**Company research leads:** VLO; MPC; PSX.

**Security check:** Provider-observed US symbols: VLO, MPC, PSX. These are economic comparisons; confirm current issuer, listing and segment perimeter before use.

**Economics and submodels:** Refiners earn product values less crude, energy and operating costs; retailers/distributors earn local fuel and merchandise margins. Renewable fuels have feedstock, credit and policy economics distinct from crude refining.

## Cross-industry relationships

These are causal research hypotheses and diagnostic links, not measured correlations or forecasts.

### Housing and household mobility

Mortgage rates and credit → transactions/starts → building products, appliances, furniture, moving/storage and brokerage.

**Relevant codes:** 25201030, 25201020, 25201040, 25504030, 40201050, 60106010, 60106020, 60108020, 60201040.

**Test:** Track permits, starts, completions and financing separately; completions create apartment supply while transactions stimulate repair and moving demand.

**Potential mistake:** Builder incentives can support new-home sales while existing-home turnover remains weak. Do not infer one broad housing recovery from a single series.

### Power delivery and computing

Funded compute demand → energized data centers → networks/chips/equipment → generation and grid investment.

**Relevant codes:** 20103010, 20104010, 20104020, 45201020, 45202030, 45301010, 45301020, 55101010, 55105010, 55105020, 60108050.

**Test:** Match signed customer commitments, grid rights, construction, equipment delivery and in-service dates. Track gross profit and funded returns at each supplier.

**Potential mistake:** A megawatt may appear in several announcements. Avoid counting the same project as independent demand and capitalizing everyone at scarcity margins.

### Oil and gas value chain

Commodity balances → producer budgets → drilling/services → transport/storage → refining and petrochemical spreads.

**Relevant codes:** 10101010, 10101020, 10102010, 10102020, 10102030, 10102040, 15101010.

**Test:** Watch actual producer cash budgets and decline replacement, not only spot prices; compare regional basis, refinery utilization and chemical feedstock spreads.

**Potential mistake:** Higher oil can help producers while hurting consumer demand or chemical margins. Contracted transport revenue may lag and retain counterparty risk.

### Agriculture and the grocery shelf

Weather/crop balances → fertilizer and farm equipment → processing/ingredients → packaged food → distributors/retailers.

**Relevant codes:** 15101030, 20106015, 30101020, 30101030, 30101040, 30202010, 30202030.

**Test:** Track crop economics, local basis, inventory, realized pricing and consumer volume; identify contract reset lags and where branded value is retained.

**Potential mistake:** Falling inputs can first reduce processors' revenue or inventory value before helping branded margins. Consumer trade-down can benefit one channel and hurt another.

### Industrial inventory cycle

End-user orders → distributor inventory → component/plant production → freight and warehousing.

**Relevant codes:** 15103020, 20107010, 20301010, 20303010, 20304010, 20304030, 45203015, 45203020, 45203030, 45301020, 60102510.

**Test:** Align sell-in, sell-through, shipments and inventories by calendar. Require orders plus actual use plus cash conversion before calling a broad recovery.

**Potential mistake:** Restocking can lift manufacturers ahead of final demand; heavier freight mix or fuel surcharge revenue can mimic unit growth.

### Medical spending and reimbursement

Payer pricing/utilization → provider staffing and collections → equipment/supplies/drugs → property operator coverage.

**Relevant codes:** 35101010, 35101020, 35102010, 35102015, 35102020, 35102030, 35202010, 60105010.

**Test:** Map who bears claims risk, who bills, who gets paid and on what lag; compare reimbursement growth with labor, acuity and denial costs.

**Potential mistake:** Higher utilization may help suppliers/providers but hurt insurers. More covered lives need not create equivalent recognized revenue or landlord rent coverage.

### Credit, refinancing and collateral

Lending standards/rates → borrower liquidity → defaults and collateral values → bank/insurer/REIT capital.

**Relevant codes:** 40101010, 40101015, 40201040, 40201050, 40202010, 40204010, 40301020, 60104010, 60201030.

**Test:** Build maturity and funding ladders, then test operating cash and collateral values together. Watch covenant headroom and required capital, not just interest coverage.

**Potential mistake:** Lower rates can relieve borrowers while compressing investment spreads or accelerating mortgage prepayments. Asset and liability duration must be matched.

### Consumer attention and distribution

Engagement/intent → advertising budgets → platforms/agencies/publishers → commerce conversion.

**Relevant codes:** 25503030, 50201010, 50201020, 50201030, 50201040, 50202010, 50202020, 50203010.

**Test:** Track advertiser outcomes, retained take rates, user retention and content costs. Distinguish distribution access from owning the customer relationship.

**Potential mistake:** Audience growth may not monetize; automated content can lower production cost while reducing scarcity and pricing for creators and intermediaries.

### Labor intensity and automation

Hiring/wage pressure → staffing and outsourced services → software/equipment substitution → customer productivity.

**Relevant codes:** 20201050, 20201070, 20202010, 20202020, 20202030, 25301040, 35102020, 45102010, 45103010, 45203010.

**Test:** Compare worker output, service quality and customer savings. Test whether suppliers retain productivity gains or pass them through in lower prices.

**Potential mistake:** A falling wage bill can signal lost volume or poor staffing rather than durable efficiency. AI spending alone does not prove customer returns.

### Insurance pricing and asset economics

Claims inflation/catastrophes → insurance pricing → broker commissions → customer expenses/property cash.

**Relevant codes:** 40301010, 40301020, 40301030, 40301040, 40301050, 60103010, 60106010, 60106020.

**Test:** Compare rate with exposure and loss trend; trace premium increases into broker revenue and insured-property expense. Check reinsurance terms and retained catastrophe risk.

**Potential mistake:** An insurer's favorable investment income does not fix inadequate reserves; rising premiums can raise broker revenue while weakening customers' occupancy or operating margins.

### Commodity substitution and resource intensity

End-use technology → metal/mineral mix → extraction/processing → equipment and infrastructure.

**Relevant codes:** 15101040, 15104010, 15104020, 15104025, 15104030, 15104040, 15104045, 15104050, 20104010, 25102010, 45301020.

**Test:** Map physical content, recovery/recycling, qualification and supply additions. Compare substitution economics at several prices rather than extrapolating current scarcity.

**Potential mistake:** A long-run demand narrative can coexist with near-term oversupply, higher extraction costs and shareholder dilution.

### Contracted growth and hidden financing

Customer contracts → working capital/construction → revenue recognition → cash collection → shareholder cash.

**Relevant codes:** 10102040, 20101010, 20103010, 35103010, 45103010, 50101010, 55101010, 55104010, 60108050.

**Test:** Record contract enforceability, termination terms, milestone timing, funding source and diluted shares. Match business perimeter across statements.

**Potential mistake:** Backlog, rate authorization, eligible lives, leased megawatts and acquisition accretion all precede cash. None independently proves funded per-share value creation.
