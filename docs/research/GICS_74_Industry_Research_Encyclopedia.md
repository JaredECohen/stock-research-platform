# 74-Industry Investment Research Encyclopedia

**Purpose:** a durable world-model for finding (1) smaller long-term compounders and (2) shorter-term mispriced inflections across the entire GICS equity universe.

Current GICS structure: **11 sectors, 25 industry groups, 74 industries, 163 sub-industries**. GICS is an organizing taxonomy; investment analysis should go below GICS whenever sub-industries have materially different economics.

## Universal research rules

1. Detect a change before searching for a stock.
2. Write the causal chain from external change to earnings and expectations.
3. Separate fact, interpretation, estimate, and unknown.
4. Classify demand quality; do not equate pipeline with contracted demand.
5. Model supply response and scarcity duration.
6. Quantify earnings elasticity.
7. Establish what is priced in before declaring a thesis variant.
8. Red-team before upgrading conviction.
9. Use industry-specific valuation and accounting checks.
10. Define dated milestones and falsifiers.


# Energy


## Energy


### Energy Equipment & Services (101010)

**Economic engine.** Oilfield activity × service intensity × day/rental rates; economics hinge on upstream capex and equipment utilization.

**Core KPIs.** Rig count; frac spreads; utilization; day rates; backlog/book-to-bill; service pricing; offshore awards; FCF conversion.

**Leading indicators.** E&P capex budgets; oil/gas strip; tender activity; rig reactivations; equipment lead times.

**Typical moats.** Technical know-how, installed fleet, high-spec equipment scarcity, customer qualification, local service network.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Very cyclical. High returns trigger fleet/equipment additions; offshore/high-spec assets can have multi-year supply lags.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF, replacement value, mid-cycle earnings.

**Accounting/data traps.** Capitalized equipment costs, maintenance vs growth capex, contract backlog quality, asset impairments.

**Common failure modes.** Overbuilding capacity, commodity collapse, customer concentration, technological substitution, leverage.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Asset-light service niches with recurring consumables/software, high switching costs, disciplined capital allocation.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Utilization crosses threshold → pricing rises → incremental margins surge; offshore tender cycle turns.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Scarcity inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** What utilization level historically unlocks pricing? How fast can idle/competing capacity return?


### Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels (101020)

**Economic engine.** Commodity price × production/refining/storage volumes − lifting/feedstock/capex; midstream adds fee-based throughput.

**Core KPIs.** Production; realized prices; breakeven; decline rate; reserves; crack spreads; throughput; inventories; FCF/bbl; leverage.

**Leading indicators.** Futures curves; inventories; OPEC/supply policy; rig activity; refinery outages; pipeline flows; LNG capacity.

**Typical moats.** Low-cost resource, advantaged acreage, scale, integrated assets, pipeline rights-of-way, long-term contracts.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Upstream/mining highly cyclical; pipelines slower; refinery capacity additions take years but utilization can swing quickly.

**Valuation lenses.** NAV, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, EV/boe/d, P/NAV; DCF for contracted midstream.

**Accounting/data traps.** Reserve estimates, hedging, asset retirement obligations, maintenance capex, working-capital swings, MLP adjustments.

**Common failure modes.** Commodity mean reversion, depletion, political/regulatory risk, leverage, poor acquisitions, stranded assets.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Low-cost reserves or fee-based infrastructure with disciplined reinvestment and per-share capital returns.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Supply deficit, refinery bottleneck, gas/LNG basis change, capex discipline creates FCF step-up.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Weekly/monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Scarcity / capital-cycle.

**Highest-EVI question:** What marginal supply sets price, and what capex is required to sustain—not grow—production?


# Materials


## Materials


### Chemicals (151010)

**Economic engine.** Price × volume − feedstock/energy; mix ranges from commodity plants to high-value formulations.

**Core KPIs.** Price/mix; volumes; utilization; feedstock spreads; inventories; EBITDA/ton; working capital; ROIC.

**Leading indicators.** Distributor inventories; plant operating rates; ethane/naphtha spreads; customer production; capacity shutdowns.

**Typical moats.** Process know-how, formulation IP, qualification, switching costs, scale, feedstock advantage.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Commodity chemicals respond via plant additions/closures; specialty capacity often less fungible and slower to qualify.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, normalized FCF, P/E, replacement cost; specialty names may warrant ROIC-based DCF.

**Accounting/data traps.** Inventory gains/losses, restructuring add-backs, environmental liabilities, pension costs, maintenance capex.

**Common failure modes.** Capacity glut, feedstock disadvantage, customer destocking, China supply, environmental liabilities.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Specialty portfolio with specification lock-in, recurring formulations, pricing power and low capital intensity.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Destocking ends → utilization/pricing recover; feedstock spread widens; competitor capacity exits.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Inventory / price-cost inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** Is the product truly differentiated or merely marketed as specialty?


### Construction Materials (151020)

**Economic engine.** Local price × volume − quarry/cement/aggregate production and freight costs; geography matters enormously.

**Core KPIs.** Tons/shipments; price/ton; aggregates margins; cement utilization; backlog; public/private construction mix.

**Leading indicators.** Infrastructure awards; housing starts; nonres construction; DOT budgets; cement imports; weather.

**Typical moats.** Permits/reserves, local scarcity, transportation economics, route density, vertical integration.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** New quarries/cement plants face permitting and long lead times; local shortages can persist for years.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, FCF, value per reserve ton, replacement value.

**Accounting/data traps.** Depletion, reclamation liabilities, acquisition accounting, weather normalization, land values.

**Common failure modes.** Construction downturn, overpaying for reserves, energy inflation, permitting changes.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Local oligopoly with scarce permitted reserves and consistent price > cost inflation.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Volume trough + continued price increases; infrastructure spend hits constrained local supply.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Local scarcity compounder.

**Highest-EVI question:** How many years of permitted reserves exist, and can competitors economically truck material into the market?


### Containers & Packaging (151030)

**Economic engine.** Units × price/mix − resin/metal/fiber and conversion cost; demand tied to consumer/industrial volumes.

**Core KPIs.** Volumes; price-cost spread; resin/paper/aluminum inputs; utilization; contract resets; EBITDA/ton/unit.

**Leading indicators.** Packaging volumes; retailer inventories; resin/pulp prices; capacity closures; customer contract renewals.

**Typical moats.** Scale, customer integration, efficient plants, local freight advantage; usually modest differentiation.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Cyclical and prone to overcapacity; closures/consolidation can restore pricing.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, normalized margins.

**Accounting/data traps.** Pass-through timing, restructuring charges, pension, lease obligations, maintenance capex.

**Common failure modes.** Overcapacity, customer bargaining power, input spikes, secular packaging substitution.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Niche packaging with high switching costs, specialized formats, disciplined consolidation.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Capacity closures + demand stabilization → price-cost spread expansion.

**Research priority:** 3/5. **Cadence:** Quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Capacity rationalization.

**Highest-EVI question:** Are price increases contractual pass-throughs or genuine pricing power?


### Metals & Mining (151040)

**Economic engine.** Commodity price × volume − cash cost − sustaining/growth capex; ore grade and mine life dominate.

**Core KPIs.** Production; realized price; cash cost/AISC; grade; recovery; reserves; mine life; capex; jurisdiction exposure.

**Leading indicators.** Spot/futures prices; inventories; Chinese demand; smelter treatment charges; mine disruptions; project approvals.

**Typical moats.** Lowest-cost resource, reserve quality, scale infrastructure, scarce geology; little product differentiation.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Extremely cyclical; new mines can require 5–15 years, making supply lags long but eventual additions powerful.

**Valuation lenses.** NAV, P/NAV, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, EV/reserve unit.

**Accounting/data traps.** Reserve assumptions, stripping capitalization, impairment, reclamation, royalties, by-product credits.

**Common failure modes.** Commodity collapse, grade decline, political expropriation/tax, project overruns, bad M&A.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Rare: royalty/streaming or exceptionally low-cost long-life assets with disciplined capital returns.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Supply disruption/deficit, inventory drawdown, capex drought, new mine ramp.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Weekly/monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Scarcity / commodity inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** Where is the marginal cost curve and what projects enter supply at today's incentive price?


### Paper & Forest Products (151050)

**Economic engine.** Pulp/lumber/paper price × volume − fiber/energy/freight; timber assets add biological growth and land optionality.

**Core KPIs.** Pulp/lumber prices; shipments; mill utilization; timber harvest; log costs; inventories; housing exposure.

**Leading indicators.** Housing starts; pulp inventories; mill downtime; export demand; freight; capacity closures.

**Typical moats.** Low-cost timberland/fiber basket, mill efficiency, local logistics; commodity moat otherwise limited.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Strong capital cycle; mill additions/closures drive multi-year pricing swings.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, timberland NAV, replacement value.

**Accounting/data traps.** Biological asset accounting, depletion, maintenance capex, pension, restructuring.

**Common failure modes.** Overcapacity, housing recession, China demand weakness, wildfire, input inflation.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Timberland with low-cost growth and disciplined harvest/land monetization.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Mill closures + demand rebound; lumber/pulp inventory cycle turns.

**Research priority:** 2/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Commodity cycle.

**Highest-EVI question:** What percentage of earnings is truly structural versus spot commodity pricing?


# Industrials


## Capital Goods


### Aerospace & Defense (201010)

**Economic engine.** Aircraft/defense installed base + production rates + aftermarket/service; defense adds funded backlog and program economics.

**Core KPIs.** Book-to-bill; backlog; aircraft deliveries; flight hours; aftermarket mix; program margin; cash conversion; defense awards.

**Leading indicators.** OEM production schedules; airline traffic; defense budgets; supplier lead times; shop visits; engine cycles.

**Typical moats.** Certification, IP, sole-source positions, installed base, long program lives, switching/requalification cost.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Capacity/qualification can take years; aftermarket scarcity can persist; defense programs span decades.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF yield, sum-of-parts; aftermarket deserves higher multiple than OE.

**Accounting/data traps.** Program accounting, customer advances, pension, warranty, development capitalization, one-time contract charges.

**Common failure modes.** Program delays, fixed-price overruns, supplier failure, leverage, aftermarket normalization.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Sole-source component/aftermarket supplier with growing installed base, high recurring content, disciplined M&A.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Production-rate recovery, aftermarket surge, defense award, supply bottleneck relief → margin step-up.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Installed-base compounder / production inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** What percentage of lifetime economics comes after the original equipment sale?


### Building Products (201020)

**Economic engine.** Residential/nonres construction volume × product content × price − materials/labor; repair/remodel can stabilize.

**Core KPIs.** Orders; backlog; price/mix; housing starts; R&R mix; dealer inventory; gross margin; channel share.

**Leading indicators.** Permits; housing starts; mortgage rates; builder orders; architectural billings; distributor inventories.

**Typical moats.** Brand/specification, contractor preference, code approval, distribution, installed base, low product cost vs labor.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Plant capacity can often be added in 1–3 years; distribution/specification barriers may matter more than factories.

**Valuation lenses.** P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF, through-cycle margins.

**Accounting/data traps.** Channel inventory, rebates, acquisition intangibles, warranty reserves, housing-cycle normalization.

**Common failure modes.** Housing downturn, destocking, raw-material inflation, private-label competition.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Mission-critical low-ticket product with contractor pull-through, recurring replacement, high incremental ROIC.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Housing/order trough + destocking end; price-cost recovers; capacity absorption.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Local/specification compounder.

**Highest-EVI question:** Is the product selected by the contractor/specifier or commoditized by the distributor?


### Construction & Engineering (201030)

**Economic engine.** Backlog conversion × project margin − labor/material overruns; cash flow depends on contract terms and working capital.

**Core KPIs.** Backlog/RPO; book-to-bill; gross margin; change orders; working capital; project mix; utilization.

**Leading indicators.** Infrastructure awards; data-center/energy project FIDs; permitting; labor availability; customer financing.

**Typical moats.** Engineering expertise, safety record, bonding capacity, customer relationships, specialized labor.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Labor and execution capacity are the bottleneck; competitors can scale but skilled teams take time.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, FCF, backlog quality, P/E; avoid valuing pass-through revenue like value-add revenue.

**Accounting/data traps.** Percentage-of-completion estimates, claims, change orders, retainage, customer advances, JV liabilities.

**Common failure modes.** Fixed-price overruns, project cancellations, labor inflation, customer insolvency.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Specialty contractor with repeat customers, low fixed capital and disciplined project selection.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Backlog surge + labor productivity + mix shift; margin normalization after troubled projects.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Backlog conversion inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** How enforceable is backlog and who bears material/labor inflation?


### Electrical Equipment (201040)

**Economic engine.** Units/capacity slots × price − copper/electrical components/labor; scarcity and qualification drive high incremental margins.

**Core KPIs.** Orders; backlog/RPO; book-to-bill; lead times; price/mix; plant utilization; data-center/grid exposure; margins.

**Leading indicators.** Utility capex; transformer/switchgear lead times; data-center FIDs; grid interconnection; copper prices.

**Typical moats.** Qualification, reliability, installed base, scarce factory slots, engineering integration, channel relationships.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** New transformer/switchgear capacity can take 2–5+ years including equipment/labor/qualification.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF; backlog-adjusted earnings and normalized margin sensitivity.

**Accounting/data traps.** Backlog definitions, advances, copper pass-through, project accounting, growth capex.

**Common failure modes.** Ghost demand, capacity glut, hyperscaler bargaining, copper inflation, execution delays.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Niche electrical supplier with scarce capacity, high reliability and recurring service/retrofit demand.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Orders outrun revenue → backlog → utilization → margin; grid/data-center bottleneck migrates into equipment.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Scarcity / backlog inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** Which orders are funded and binding, and when does new industry capacity arrive?


### Industrial Conglomerates (201050)

**Economic engine.** Portfolio cash flows plus capital allocation; value depends on segment quality and management's reinvestment choices.

**Core KPIs.** Segment margins/ROIC; organic growth; FCF; acquisition multiples; portfolio mix; leverage.

**Leading indicators.** Portfolio reviews; spin-offs; acquisitions; segment order trends; capital allocation changes.

**Typical moats.** Capital allocation system, decentralized culture, shared distribution/technology; conglomerate discount may or may not be justified.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Depends on segments; acquisition capital cycle can become the key constraint.

**Valuation lenses.** SOTP, FCF yield, EV/EBITDA, ROIC; compare implied segment multiples.

**Accounting/data traps.** Corporate allocations, pension, intercompany items, acquisition adjustments, stranded costs.

**Common failure modes.** Empire building, opaque segments, leverage, bad M&A, conglomerate discount persistence.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Decentralized operator with repeatable acquisition playbook and high incremental ROIC.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Spin/separation reveals higher-quality asset; margin reset; capital allocation regime changes.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** SOTP / capital-allocation.

**Highest-EVI question:** Does the corporate center create value beyond what independent segments could?


### Machinery (201060)

**Economic engine.** Equipment units × price + aftermarket − manufacturing cost; highly sensitive to utilization and customer capex.

**Core KPIs.** Orders; book-to-bill; backlog; dealer inventories; utilization; price/cost; aftermarket mix; incremental margin.

**Leading indicators.** PMI; construction/mining/ag equipment demand; dealer inventory; commodity farmer income; capex plans.

**Typical moats.** Installed base, dealer network, application expertise, brand, parts/service ecosystem.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Factories respond in 1–3 years, but dealer/installed-base moats persist; end markets can be deeply cyclical.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF, normalized mid-cycle margins.

**Accounting/data traps.** Dealer financing, inventory, warranty, pension, restructuring, acquisition intangibles.

**Common failure modes.** Dealer destocking, peak-cycle extrapolation, China competition, input inflation.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Niche machinery with consumables/service, high aftermarket share, low customer failure tolerance.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Dealer inventory bottom, utilization rebound, price-cost turn, new product qualification.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Operating leverage / installed base.

**Highest-EVI question:** How much gross profit comes from aftermarket versus new machines?


### Trading Companies & Distributors (201070)

**Economic engine.** Volume × gross margin − logistics/SG&A; working-capital turns and local scale are central.

**Core KPIs.** Organic sales; gross margin; inventory turns; working capital; branch productivity; private-label mix; ROIC.

**Leading indicators.** Industrial production; channel inventories; supplier lead times; customer order frequency; pricing.

**Typical moats.** Distribution density, breadth, availability, procurement scale, embedded workflow, private label.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Physical capacity is easy; network density, data and relationships compound slowly.

**Valuation lenses.** P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF, ROIC; working-capital-normalized FCF.

**Accounting/data traps.** LIFO/FIFO, inventory gains, supplier rebates, acquisition add-backs, receivables.

**Common failure modes.** Destocking, e-commerce disintermediation, inventory obsolescence, acquisition overpayment.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Specialty distributor with fragmented market, high service content, private label and bolt-on runway.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Destocking ends; gross margin recovers; working capital releases cash.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Density compounder / destocking inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** What value does the distributor add that a manufacturer or online marketplace cannot replicate?


## Commercial & Professional Services


### Commercial Services & Supplies (202010)

**Economic engine.** Recurring service contracts × route/site density − labor/equipment; includes waste, facilities, security, office services.

**Core KPIs.** Organic price; retention; route density; labor utilization; recurring mix; acquisition multiples; FCF.

**Leading indicators.** Contract renewals; wage inflation; disposal pricing; customer formation; regulation; acquisition pipeline.

**Typical moats.** Route density, permits, local regulation, installed systems, switching friction, reputation.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Local networks take years to build; attractive returns invite bolt-on consolidation more than greenfield entry.

**Valuation lenses.** P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, ROIC; route businesses often deserve DCF on local density economics.

**Accounting/data traps.** Acquisition adjustments, environmental reserves, landfill closure, lease classification, labor normalization.

**Common failure modes.** Labor inflation, contract repricing lag, poor M&A, environmental liabilities.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Waste/environmental/security niche with high retention, route density, pricing > inflation.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Price catches wages; route density improves; competitor exits; acquisition synergies.

**Research priority:** 5/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Local-density compounder.

**Highest-EVI question:** Can price rise annually without meaningful churn?


### Professional Services (202020)

**Economic engine.** Billable headcount/utilization × billing rate − compensation; data/process outsourcing adds recurring contracts.

**Core KPIs.** Utilization; bill rates; headcount; attrition; bookings; backlog; recurring mix; revenue per employee.

**Leading indicators.** Hiring trends; wage inflation; client budgets; consulting demand; outsourcing awards; regulation.

**Typical moats.** Reputation, specialized expertise, proprietary data, workflow integration, switching costs.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Human capital supply responds relatively quickly; proprietary data/workflow moats are more durable.

**Valuation lenses.** P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF; revenue-per-employee and organic growth quality.

**Accounting/data traps.** Capitalized software, stock comp, acquisition earnouts, billable vs non-billable headcount.

**Common failure modes.** Utilization collapse, wage pressure, automation/AI, client concentration.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Vertical data/analytics or outsourced mission-critical process with recurring contracts and high retention.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Hiring freeze ends, utilization rebounds, pricing outpaces wages, large outsourcing award.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Utilization / recurring data.

**Highest-EVI question:** Is the moat people-based or embedded in proprietary data/workflows?


## Transportation


### Air Freight & Logistics (203010)

**Economic engine.** Shipment volume × yield/spread − transport procurement/labor; asset-light brokers differ from asset-heavy integrators.

**Core KPIs.** Tonnage; yield; load factor; purchased transportation; spot vs contract rates; working capital.

**Leading indicators.** Freight indices; e-commerce; air cargo rates; inventories; port volumes; fuel.

**Typical moats.** Network density, customs capability, scale purchasing, customer integration, route network.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Broker capacity adjusts fast; integrated global networks are slow and capital intensive.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF; mid-cycle margins.

**Accounting/data traps.** Fuel surcharges, purchased transportation gross vs net reporting, leases, pension.

**Common failure modes.** Rate collapse, excess capacity, labor, fuel, customer concentration.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Asset-light logistics with sticky vertical specialization and technology-enabled network effects.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Freight recession ends → rates/volumes recover; purchased transport spread expands.

**Research priority:** 3/5. **Cadence:** Weekly/monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Freight-cycle inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** Are profits driven by structural network advantage or temporary rate spreads?


### Passenger Airlines (203020)

**Economic engine.** Passengers × fare/yield − fuel/labor/aircraft ownership; high fixed costs create extreme operating leverage.

**Core KPIs.** RASM/PRASM; CASM ex-fuel; load factor; capacity; bookings; fare; loyalty economics; net debt.

**Leading indicators.** TSA/booking data; capacity schedules; jet fuel; competitor cuts; corporate travel.

**Typical moats.** Slots, hubs, loyalty programs, scale; historically weak due to low switching cost and high fixed costs.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Aircraft/crew capacity adjusts slowly but bankruptcies/restructurings reset economics.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDAR, FCF, normalized EPS; value loyalty separately when material.

**Accounting/data traps.** Aircraft leases, loyalty deferred revenue, pension, special items, maintenance timing.

**Common failure modes.** Fare wars, fuel spikes, labor contracts, leverage, recession, aircraft grounding.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Rare; loyalty/network with disciplined capacity and strong balance sheet.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Capacity cuts + resilient demand → fare/yield inflection; fuel decline.

**Research priority:** 2/5. **Cadence:** Weekly/monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Capacity rationalization.

**Highest-EVI question:** Is capacity discipline industry-wide or temporary?


### Marine Transportation (203030)

**Economic engine.** Freight rate × vessel days − fuel/opex; ship supply orderbook and route demand drive economics.

**Core KPIs.** Spot/contract rates; utilization; fleet age; orderbook; scrapping; ton-miles; breakeven.

**Leading indicators.** Orderbook deliveries; port congestion; trade flows; canal constraints; commodity demand.

**Typical moats.** Low-cost fleet, niche routes/contracts; otherwise weak moat.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Very strong capital cycle: high rates trigger ship orders with 2–4 year delivery lag.

**Valuation lenses.** NAV, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield, fleet value.

**Accounting/data traps.** Vessel values, depreciation, charter accounting, drydock capex.

**Common failure modes.** Fleet oversupply, rate collapse, leverage, geopolitics, environmental capex.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Niche contracted operator with scarce specialized vessels and disciplined fleet growth.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Orderbook tight + demand/ton-mile shock; congestion/geopolitical rerouting.

**Research priority:** 3/5. **Cadence:** Weekly. **Preferred archetype:** Shipping supply-cycle.

**Highest-EVI question:** What percentage of fleet capacity is already on order relative to demand growth?


### Ground Transportation (203040)

**Economic engine.** Rail/truck/bus volume × yield − labor/fuel/equipment; network density and capacity utilization determine margins.

**Core KPIs.** Carloads/loads; yield; OR; miles; driver/crew productivity; spot/contract rates; equipment utilization.

**Leading indicators.** Freight indices; inventory cycle; industrial production; truck exits; rail service metrics.

**Typical moats.** Rail network/rights-of-way; terminal density; brokerage network; dedicated contracts.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Truck capacity adjusts quickly through exits; rail infrastructure is effectively impossible to replicate.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF; rail normalized OR and capex.

**Accounting/data traps.** Equipment leases, fuel surcharges, pension, owner-operator classification.

**Common failure modes.** Freight recession, labor, regulation, service failure, capacity additions.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Rail/network or niche fleet with density and pricing power.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Truck capacity exits → rates turn; rail service improvement unlocks price/volume.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Weekly/monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Capacity / network moat.

**Highest-EVI question:** What is the marginal carrier's cash breakeven and exit rate?


### Transportation Infrastructure (203050)

**Economic engine.** Passenger/cargo throughput × regulated/concession fees − operating/capex; often toll-road-like.

**Core KPIs.** Traffic; passengers; TEUs; aeronautical/non-aero revenue; tariff increases; capex; concession life.

**Leading indicators.** Travel volumes; trade; port congestion; government concessions; route additions.

**Typical moats.** Scarce concession, location, network, regulatory barriers.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Very long-lived, difficult-to-replicate assets; expansions slow and regulated.

**Valuation lenses.** DCF, EV/EBITDA, concession NAV, dividend yield.

**Accounting/data traps.** Concession liabilities, capitalized interest, regulatory assets, maintenance capex.

**Common failure modes.** Traffic shock, political tariff controls, concession renewal, leverage.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Long concession with inflation-linked pricing and growing throughput.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Traffic recovery; tariff reset; capacity expansion commissioned.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Infrastructure toll-road.

**Highest-EVI question:** How much pricing is contractual versus politically discretionary?


# Consumer Discretionary


## Automobiles & Components


### Automobile Components (251010)

**Economic engine.** Vehicle production × content per vehicle × price − materials/labor; EV/ADAS can radically shift content.

**Core KPIs.** Global light vehicle production; content/vehicle; backlog; customer mix; margin; launches; warranty.

**Leading indicators.** OEM production schedules; vehicle inventories; EV penetration; platform awards; semiconductor supply.

**Typical moats.** Qualification, platform lock-in, safety certification, engineering integration; OEM bargaining limits returns.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Plant/tooling additions tied to programs; multi-year awards but rapid technology shifts.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF; normalize cycle and launch costs.

**Accounting/data traps.** Tooling reimbursements, warranty, restructuring, pension, program capitalized costs.

**Common failure modes.** OEM price-downs, platform loss, EV transition, recall, leverage.

**Ideal compounder setup.** High-content niche supplier with IP, aftermarket or safety-critical products and diversified platforms.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Production recovery + new content launch + fixed-cost absorption.

**Research priority:** 4/5. **Cadence:** Monthly. **Preferred archetype:** Content-per-unit inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** Does content growth outweigh annual OEM price-downs and EV substitution risk?


### Automobiles (251020)

**Economic engine.** Vehicle units × ASP − materials/labor/warranty + financing/software; enormous fixed capital and product-cycle risk.

**Core KPIs.** Deliveries; ASP; incentives; inventory days; utilization; gross margin; FCF; warranty; finance penetration.

**Leading indicators.** Dealer inventory; incentives; order books; battery/raw materials; rates; used-car prices.

**Typical moats.** Brand, scale, dealer/service network, software ecosystem, manufacturing efficiency.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Capacity additions are large and slow; price wars can destroy returns rapidly.

**Valuation lenses.** EV/EBITDA, P/E, FCF, sum-of-parts, enterprise value per unit/capacity.

### Real Estate Management & Development (602010)

**Economic engine.** Development spread, management/brokerage fees, transaction volumes and owned-property cash flows.

**Core KPIs.** Pre-sales; backlog; land bank; development margin; AUM; fee revenue; transactions; net debt.

**Leading indicators.** Cap rates; mortgage rates; transaction volumes; permits; property prices.

**Typical moats.** Local land expertise, brand, capital relationships, management scale, data.

**Capital cycle / supply response.** Development supply lags but can overbuild; fee businesses scale faster.

**Valuation lenses.** NAV/SOTP, P/E for fee businesses, P/B for developers.

**Accounting/data traps.** Land valuation, capitalized interest, JV/off-balance-sheet debt, percentage-of-completion.

**Common failure modes.** Property downturn, leverage, land impairments, transaction freeze.

**Ideal compounder setup.** Asset-light manager/service platform with recurring AUM and low capital needs.

**Ideal inflection setup.** Transaction cycle reopens; distressed land bought cheaply; pre-sales turn.

**Research priority:** 3/5. **Cadence:** Monthly/quarterly. **Preferred archetype:** Transaction / NAV inflection.

**Highest-EVI question:** How much value is fee-based recurring earnings versus leveraged land/property exposure?
